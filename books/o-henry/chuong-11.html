O. HENRY · 30 TRUYỆN NGẮN · BÀI ĐỌC KÈM TỪ VỰNG

Springtime à la Carte

Truyện 11 · Xuân về trên thực đơn · 2.175 chữ · 129 từ

Từ in đậm được giải nghĩa ở cột bên phải. Chạm từ in đậm để tô sáng phần giải nghĩa. Mỗi truyện giải nghĩa độc lập.

BÀI ĐỌC
TỪ VỰNG

It was a day in March.

Never, never begin a story this way when you write one. No opening could possibly be worse. It is unimaginative, flat, dry and likely to consist of mere wind. But in this instance it is allowable. For the following paragraph, which should have inaugurated the narrative, is too wildly extravagant and preposterous to be flaunted in the face of the reader without preparation.

  • mere wind (cụm từ)chỉ toàn gió; chơi chữ: “lời rỗng tuếch” và “gió tháng Ba”
  • inaugurate (v) /ɪˈnɔːɡjəreɪt/khai mạc, mở đầu (chữ trang trọng)
  • extravagant (adj) /ɪkˈstrævəɡənt/ngông, quá đáng
  • preposterous (adj) /prɪˈpɒstərəs/phi lý, lố bịch

Sarah was crying over her bill of fare.

Think of a New York girl shedding tears on the menu card!

  • bill of fare (cụm từ)thực đơn (cách nói cũ)

To account for this you will be allowed to guess that the lobsters were all out, or that she had sworn ice-cream off during Lent, or that she had ordered onions, or that she had just come from a Hackett matinee. And then, all these theories being wrong, you will please let the story proceed.

  • swear off (cụm động từ)thề kiêng, thề bỏ hẳn
  • Lent (n) /lent/Mùa Chay (40 ngày trước lễ Phục sinh), người Công giáo kiêng ăn uống
  • Hackett (n) /ˈhækɪt/James K. Hackett, tài tử kịch lãng mạn được các cô gái mê thời đó
  • matinee (n) /ˈmætɪneɪ/suất diễn ban chiều

The gentleman who announced that the world was an oyster which he with his sword would open made a larger hit than he deserved. It is not difficult to open an oyster with a sword. But did you ever notice anyone try to open the terrestrial bivalve with a typewriter? Like to wait for a dozen raw opened that way?

  • the world is one’s oyster (thành ngữ)thế giới là con hàu của ta: cả thế gian trong tay mình. Câu trong kịch Shakespeare
  • terrestrial (adj) /təˈrestriəl/thuộc trái đất
  • bivalve (n) /ˈbaɪvælv/loài thân mềm hai mảnh vỏ (như hàu). “terrestrial bivalve” = cách gọi to tát chỉ “con hàu thế giới”
  • typewriter (n) /ˈtaɪpraɪtə/máy chữ. Ở đoạn sau còn mang nghĩa cũ: người đánh máy (nay gọi typist)

Sarah had managed to pry apart the shells with her unhandy weapon far enough to nibble a wee bit at the cold and clammy world within. She knew no more shorthand than if she had been a graduate in stenography just let slip upon the world by a business college. So, not being able to stenog, she could not enter that bright galaxy of office talent. She was a freelance typewriter and canvassed for odd jobs of copying.

  • unhandy (adj) /ʌnˈhændi/vụng, khó dùng
  • wee (adj) /wiː/nhỏ xíu (giọng Scotland); “a wee bit” = một chút xíu
  • stenography (n) /stəˈnɒɡrəfi/nghề tốc ký
  • stenog (v) /stəˈnɒɡ/chữ tự chế để đùa: “làm tốc ký”
  • galaxy (n) /ˈɡæləksi/dải ngân hà; nghĩa trong bài: đám người tài giỏi, rực rỡ
  • freelance (adj) /ˈfriːlɑːns/làm tự do, không thuộc cơ quan nào
  • canvass (v) /ˈkænvəs/đi chào mời, đi kiếm (việc, khách)

The most brilliant and crowning feat of Sarah’s battle with the world was the deal she made with Schulenberg’s Home Restaurant. The restaurant was next door to the old red brick in which she hall-roomed. One evening after dining at Schulenberg’s 40-cent, five-course table d’hôte (served as fast as you throw the five baseballs at the coloured gentleman’s head) Sarah took away with her the bill of fare. It was written in an almost unreadable script neither English nor German, and so arranged that if you were not careful you began with a toothpick and rice pudding and ended with soup and the day of the week.

  • feat (n) /fiːt/chiến công, kỳ tích
  • red brick (cụm từ)nhà gạch đỏ (dãy nhà trọ kiểu cũ)
  • hall-room (v) /ˈhɔːl ruːm/ở trọ trong phòng hành lang (phòng nhỏ rẻ nhất ở cuối hành lang)
  • table d’hôte (cụm từ)(tiếng Pháp) bữa ăn trọn gói nhiều món, giá cố định
  • throw baseballs at the head (cụm từ)trò chơi hội chợ thời đó: ném bóng trúng đầu người da đen thò qua lỗ màn. Ý: món ăn dọn ra dồn dập
  • script (n) /skrɪpt/nghĩa trong bài: chữ viết tay

The next day Sarah showed Schulenberg a neat card on which the menu was beautifully typewritten with the viands temptingly marshalled under their right and proper heads from “hors d’oeuvre” to “not responsible for overcoats and umbrellas.”

  • viand (n) /ˈvaɪənd/(văn cổ) món ăn
  • marshal (v) /ˈmɑːʃl/sắp xếp thành hàng ngũ (như dàn quân)
  • head (n) /hed/nghĩa trong bài: mục, đề mục
  • hors d’oeuvre (cụm từ)(tiếng Pháp) món khai vị

Schulenberg became a naturalised citizen on the spot. Before Sarah left him she had him willingly committed to an agreement. She was to furnish typewritten bills of fare for the twenty-one tables in the restaurant—a new bill for each day’s dinner, and new ones for breakfast and lunch as often as changes occurred in the food or as neatness required.

  • naturalised citizen (cụm từ)người nhập quốc tịch. Ý đùa: ông chủ người Đức phục đến mức “thành người Mỹ” ngay
  • commit (v) /kəˈmɪt/nghĩa trong bài: ràng buộc vào (một thỏa thuận)

In return for this Schulenberg was to send three meals per diem to Sarah’s hall room by a waiter—an obsequious one if possible—and furnish her each afternoon with a pencil draft of what Fate had in store for Schulenberg’s customers on the morrow.

  • per diem (cụm từ)(tiếng Latin) mỗi ngày
  • obsequious (adj) /əbˈsiːkwiəs/khúm núm, xun xoe
  • in store for (cụm từ)đang chờ sẵn (ai)
  • morrow (n) /ˈmɒrəʊ/(văn cổ) ngày mai

Mutual satisfaction resulted from the agreement. Schulenberg’s patrons now knew what the food they ate was called even if its nature sometimes puzzled them. And Sarah had food during a cold, dull winter, which was the main thing with her.

  • patron (n) /ˈpeɪtrən/khách quen

And then the almanac lied, and said that spring had come. Spring comes when it comes. The frozen snows of January still lay like adamant in the crosstown streets. The hand-organs still played “In the Good Old Summertime,” with their December vivacity and expression. Men began to make thirty-day notes to buy Easter dresses. Janitors shut off steam. And when these things happen one may know that the city is still in the clutches of winter.

  • almanac (n) /ˈɔːlmənæk/lịch niên giám (có ghi thời tiết, mùa)
  • adamant (n) /ˈædəmənt/(văn cổ) đá cứng như kim cương
  • crosstown (adj) /ˈkrɒstaʊn/chạy ngang thành phố (từ đông sang tây)
  • hand-organ (n) /ˈhænd ɔːɡən/đàn quay tay của người hát rong
  • In the Good Old Summertime (cụm từ)tên một bài hát rất thịnh năm 1902 (“Mùa hè xưa êm đềm”)
  • vivacity (n) /vɪˈvæsəti/sự sôi nổi, hoạt bát
  • thirty-day note (cụm từ)giấy nợ hẹn trả sau 30 ngày
  • janitor (n) /ˈdʒænɪtə/người gác, quản lý tòa nhà
  • steam (n) /stiːm/nghĩa trong bài: hơi nóng sưởi ấm (lò sưởi hơi nước)
  • clutch (n) /klʌtʃ/nanh vuốt; “in the clutches of” = trong tay, bị kìm kẹp

One afternoon Sarah shivered in her elegant hall bedroom; “house heated; scrupulously clean; conveniences; seen to be appreciated.” She had no work to do except Schulenberg’s menu cards. Sarah sat in her squeaky willow rocker, and looked out the window. The calendar on the wall kept crying to her: “Springtime is here, Sarah—springtime is here, I tell you. Look at me, Sarah, my figures show it. You’ve got a neat figure yourself, Sarah—a—nice springtime figure—why do you look out the window so sadly?”

  • scrupulously (adv) /ˈskruːpjələsli/cực kỳ cẩn thận, tỉ mỉ. Câu trong ngoặc nhái lời quảng cáo cho thuê phòng
  • seen to be appreciated (cụm từ)lời quảng cáo nhà trọ: “phải xem tận mắt mới thấy hết cái hay”
  • willow rocker (cụm từ)ghế bập bênh đan bằng cành liễu
  • figure (n) /ˈfɪɡə/chơi chữ: “con số” trên tờ lịch và “vóc dáng” của cô gái

Sarah’s room was at the back of the house. Looking out the window she could see the windowless rear brick wall of the box factory on the next street. But the wall was clearest crystal; and Sarah was looking down a grassy lane shaded with cherry trees and elms and bordered with raspberry bushes and Cherokee roses.

  • Cherokee rose (cụm từ)hoa hồng Cherokee, hồng dại hoa trắng ở miền Nam nước Mỹ

Spring’s real harbingers are too subtle for the eye and ear. Some must have the flowering crocus, the wood-starring dogwood, the voice of bluebird—even so gross a reminder as the farewell handshake of the retiring buckwheat and oyster before they can welcome the Lady in Green to their dull bosoms. But to old earth’s choicest kin there come straight, sweet messages from his newest bride, telling them they shall be no stepchildren unless they choose to be.

On the previous summer Sarah had gone into the country and loved a farmer.

  • harbinger (n) /ˈhɑːbɪndʒə/điềm báo, sứ giả báo trước
  • crocus (n) /ˈkrəʊkəs/hoa nghệ tây, nở sớm đầu xuân
  • dogwood (n) /ˈdɒɡwʊd/cây sơn thù du, nở hoa trắng đầu xuân như sao trong rừng
  • gross (adj) /ɡrəʊs/nghĩa trong bài: thô thiển, trần tục
  • buckwheat (n) /ˈbʌkwiːt/kiều mạch; bánh kiều mạch là món ăn sáng mùa đông
  • the Lady in Green (cụm từ)“Nàng áo xanh”: cách gọi mùa xuân
  • bosom (n) /ˈbʊzəm/lồng ngực, tấm lòng (văn chương)
  • kin (n) /kɪn/họ hàng, người thân

(In writing your story never hark back thus. It is bad art, and cripples interest. Let it march, march.)

  • hark back (cụm động từ)quay lại chuyện cũ
  • cripple (v) /ˈkrɪpl/làm tê liệt, làm hỏng

Sarah stayed two weeks at Sunnybrook Farm. There she learned to love old Farmer Franklin’s son Walter. Farmers have been loved and wedded and turned out to grass in less time. But young Walter Franklin was a modern agriculturist. He had a telephone in his cow house, and he could figure up exactly what effect next year’s Canada wheat crop would have on potatoes planted in the dark of the moon.

  • turn out to grass (thành ngữ)thả ra đồng cỏ (như ngựa già); ý đùa: cho về vườn
  • agriculturist (n) /ˌæɡrɪˈkʌltʃərɪst/nhà nông học, người làm nông có học
  • in the dark of the moon (cụm từ)vào kỳ trăng tối; nông dân xưa tin trồng khoai lúc ấy thì tốt

It was in this shaded and raspberried lane that Walter had wooed and won her. And together they had sat and woven a crown of dandelions for her hair. He had immoderately praised the effect of the yellow blossoms against her brown tresses; and she had left the chaplet there, and walked back to the house swinging her straw sailor in her hands.

  • raspberried (adj) /ˈrɑːzbərid/chữ tự chế: rợp bụi mâm xôi
  • woo (v) /wuː/tán tỉnh, theo đuổi
  • dandelion (n) /ˈdændɪlaɪən/hoa bồ công anh (hoa vàng; lá non ăn như rau)
  • immoderately (adv) /ɪˈmɒdərətli/quá mức, không chừng mực
  • tress (n) /tres/mái tóc dài (văn chương)
  • chaplet (n) /ˈtʃæplət/vòng hoa đội đầu
  • straw sailor (cụm từ)mũ rơm kiểu thủy thủ, chóp phẳng vành cứng

They were to marry in the spring—at the very first signs of spring, Walter said. And Sarah came back to the city to pound her typewriter.

  • pound (v) /paʊnd/nghĩa trong bài: gõ mạnh, nện (phím máy chữ)

A knock at the door dispelled Sarah’s visions of that happy day. A waiter had brought the rough pencil draft of the Home Restaurant’s next day fare in old Schulenberg’s angular hand.

Sarah sat down to her typewriter and slipped a card between the rollers. She was a nimble worker. Generally in an hour and a half the twenty-one menu cards were written and ready.

  • dispel (v) /dɪˈspel/xua tan, làm tiêu tan
  • angular (adj) /ˈæŋɡjələ/nhiều góc cạnh; “angular hand” = chữ viết tay gãy gọn, cứng

Today there were more changes on the bill of fare than usual. The soups were lighter; pork was eliminated from the entrées, figuring only with Russian turnips among the roasts. The gracious spirit of spring pervaded the entire menu. Lamb, that lately capered on the greening hillsides, was becoming exploited with the sauce that commemorated its gambols. The song of the oyster, though not silenced, was dimuendo con amore. The frying-pan seemed to be held, inactive, behind the beneficent bars of the broiler. The pie list swelled; the richer puddings had vanished; the sausage, with his drapery wrapped about him, barely lingered in a pleasant thanatopsis with the buckwheats and the sweet but doomed maple.

  • entrée (n) /ˈɒntreɪ/món chính
  • pervade (v) /pəˈveɪd/lan tỏa khắp
  • caper (v) /ˈkeɪpə/nhảy nhót tung tăng
  • exploit (v) /ɪkˈsplɔɪt/khai thác, đem ra dùng
  • commemorate (v) /kəˈmeməreɪt/tưởng nhớ, kỷ niệm. Đùa: nước sốt bạc hà dùng với thịt cừu như để “tưởng niệm” cừu non
  • gambol (n) /ˈɡæmbl/những cú nhảy cỡn
  • diminuendo con amore (cụm từ)(thuật ngữ nhạc, tiếng Ý) nhỏ dần, đầy yêu thương. Ý: món hàu thưa dần khi sang xuân
  • beneficent (adj) /bɪˈnefɪsnt/nhân từ, làm điều tốt
  • broiler (n) /ˈbrɔɪlə/lò nướng, vỉ nướng
  • drapery (n) /ˈdreɪpəri/tấm vải phủ; ở đây là lớp vỏ bọc xúc xích
  • thanatopsis (n) /ˌθænəˈtɒpsɪs/suy niệm về cái chết (tên bài thơ nổi tiếng của Bryant). Chữ to tát để đùa về món xúc xích sắp bị gạch khỏi thực đơn
  • doomed (adj) /duːmd/số đã tận, sắp bị loại
  • maple (n) /ˈmeɪpl/cây phong; ở đây là xi-rô phong rưới bánh kiều mạch

Sarah’s fingers danced like midgets above a summer stream. Down through the courses she worked, giving each item its position according to its length with an accurate eye. Just above the desserts came the list of vegetables. Carrots and peas, asparagus on toast, the perennial tomatoes and corn and succotash, lima beans, cabbage—and then—

  • midget (n) /ˈmɪdʒɪt/nghĩa trong bài: muỗi nhỏ, bọ nhỏ
  • succotash (n) /ˈsʌkətæʃ/món ngô hạt nấu đậu của người Mỹ

Sarah was crying over her bill of fare. Tears from the depths of some divine despair rose in her heart and gathered to her eyes. Down went her head on the little typewriter stand; and the keyboard rattled a dry accompaniment to her moist sobs.

  • divine despair (cụm từ)nỗi tuyệt vọng thiêng liêng (mượn thơ Tennyson “Tears, idle tears”)
  • accompaniment (n) /əˈkʌmpənimənt/phần đệm (nhạc)

For she had received no letter from Walter in two weeks, and the next item on the bill of fare was dandelions—dandelions with some kind of egg—but bother the egg!—dandelions, with whose golden blooms Walter had crowned her his queen of love and future bride—dandelions, the harbingers of spring, her sorrow’s crown of sorrow—reminder of her happiest days.

  • bother (v) /ˈbɒðə/thán từ: mặc kệ, kệ (quả trứng)!
  • sorrow’s crown of sorrow (cụm từ)nỗi đau nhất trong các nỗi đau: nhớ lại ngày hạnh phúc lúc đang buồn (thơ Tennyson)

Madam, I dare you to smile until you suffer this test: Let the Marechal Niel roses that Percy brought you on the night you gave him your heart be served as a salad with French dressing before your eyes at a Schulenberg table d’hôte. Had Juliet so seen her love tokens dishonoured the sooner would she have sought the lethean herbs of the good apothecary.

  • Maréchal Niel (n) /ˌmæreʃæl ˈniːl/giống hoa hồng vàng thơm nổi tiếng thời đó
  • French dressing (cụm từ)nước sốt dầu giấm trộn sa-lát
  • token (n) /ˈtəʊkən/vật làm tin, kỷ vật
  • dishonour (v) /dɪsˈɒnə/làm ô nhục, xúc phạm
  • lethean (adj) /ˈliːθiən/thuộc sông Lethe (sông quên lãng trong thần thoại Hy Lạp); làm quên hết
  • apothecary (n) /əˈpɒθəkəri/(cổ) người bán thuốc, dược sĩ. Gợi chuyện Romeo và Juliet

But what a witch is Spring! Into the great cold city of stone and iron a message had to be sent. There was none to convey it but the little hardy courier of the fields with his rough green coat and modest air. He is a true soldier of fortune, this dent-de-lion—this lion’s tooth, as the French chefs call him. Flowered, he will assist at lovemaking, wreathed in my lady’s nut-brown hair; young and callow and unblossomed, he goes into the boiling pot and delivers the word of his sovereign mistress.

  • hardy (adj) /ˈhɑːdi/chịu đựng giỏi, dẻo dai
  • courier (n) /ˈkʊriə/người đưa thư, sứ giả
  • soldier of fortune (thành ngữ)lính đánh thuê, kẻ phiêu lưu
  • dent-de-lion (cụm từ)(tiếng Pháp) “răng sư tử”, gốc của chữ dandelion
  • wreathe (v) /riːð/kết vòng, quấn quanh
  • callow (adj) /ˈkæləʊ/non nớt, còn non
  • sovereign (adj) /ˈsɒvrɪn/tối cao; “sovereign mistress” = bà chúa (ở đây là mùa xuân)

By and by Sarah forced back her tears. The cards must be written. But, still in a faint, golden glow from her dandeleonine dream, she fingered the typewriter keys absently for a little while, with her mind and heart in the meadow lane with her young farmer. But soon she came swiftly back to the rockbound lanes of Manhattan, and the typewriter began to rattle and jump like a strikebreaker’s motor car.

  • by and by (cụm từ)một lúc sau, dần dần
  • dandeleonine (adj) /ˌdændɪˈliːənaɪn/chữ tự chế: dandelion + leonine (thuộc sư tử)
  • rockbound (adj) /ˈrɒkbaʊnd/toàn đá bao quanh
  • strikebreaker (n) /ˈstraɪkbreɪkə/kẻ phá đình công (làm thay người đình công); xe họ phải chạy gấp tránh bị ném đá

At 6 o’clock the waiter brought her dinner and carried away the typewritten bill of fare. When Sarah ate she set aside, with a sigh, the dish of dandelions with its crowning ovarious accompaniment. As this dark mass had been transformed from a bright and love-endorsed flower to be an ignominious vegetable, so had her summer hopes wilted and perished. Love may, as Shakespeare said, feed on itself: but Sarah could not bring herself to eat the dandelions that had graced, as ornaments, the first spiritual banquet of her heart’s true affection.

  • ovarious (adj) /əʊˈveəriəs/chữ hiếm, nói đùa: thuộc về trứng
  • endorse (v) /ɪnˈdɔːs/tán thành, chứng nhận
  • ignominious (adj) /ˌɪɡnəˈmɪniəs/nhục nhã, hèn mọn

At 7:30 the couple in the next room began to quarrel: the man in the room above sought for A on his flute; the gas went a little lower; three coal wagons started to unload—the only sound of which the phonograph is jealous; cats on the back fences slowly retreated toward Mukden. By these signs Sarah knew that it was time for her to read. She got out The Cloister and the Hearth, the best non-selling book of the month, settled her feet on her trunk, and began to wander with Gerard.

  • phonograph (n) /ˈfəʊnəɡrɑːf/máy hát đĩa (đời đầu)
  • Mukden (n) /ˈmʊkdən/Thẩm Dương (Trung Quốc), nơi quân Nga rút lui năm 1905. Ý đùa: lũ mèo rút lui như quân bại trận
  • The Cloister and the Hearth (cụm từ)“Tu viện và mái ấm”, tiểu thuyết của Charles Reade; Gerard và Denys là hai nhân vật
  • non-selling (adj) /ˌnɒn ˈselɪŋ/bán không chạy. Đùa ngược với “best-selling”

The front door bell rang. The landlady answered it. Sarah left Gerard and Denys treed by a bear and listened. Oh, yes; you would, just as she did!

  • tree (v) /triː/đuổi (ai) phải trèo lên cây

And then a strong voice was heard in the hall below, and Sarah jumped for her door, leaving the book on the floor and the first round easily the bear’s. You have guessed it. She reached the top of the stairs just as her farmer came up, three at a jump, and reaped and garnered her, with nothing left for the gleaners.

“Why haven’t you written—oh, why?” cried Sarah.

  • garner (v) /ˈɡɑːnə/thu vào kho
  • gleaner (n) /ˈɡliːnə/người mót lúa. Ví von nhà nông: gặt sạch, không để gì cho người mót

“New York is a pretty large town,” said Walter Franklin. “I came in a week ago to your old address. I found that you went away on a Thursday. That consoled some; it eliminated the possible Friday bad luck. But it didn’t prevent my hunting for you with police and otherwise ever since!”

“I wrote!” said Sarah, vehemently.

“Never got it!”

“Then how did you find me?”

The young farmer smiled a springtime smile.

  • Friday bad luck (cụm từ)người Mỹ xưa tin thứ Sáu là ngày xui

“I dropped into that Home Restaurant next door this evening,” said he. “I don’t care who knows it; I like a dish of some kind of greens at this time of the year. I ran my eye down that nice typewritten bill of fare looking for something in that line. When I got below cabbage I turned my chair over and hollered for the proprietor. He told me where you lived.”

“I remember,” sighed Sarah, happily. “That was dandelions below cabbage.”

  • greens (n) /ɡriːnz/rau xanh, rau lá ăn được
  • proprietor (n) /prəˈpraɪətə/chủ (quán)

“I’d know that cranky capital W ’way above the line that your typewriter makes anywhere in the world,” said Franklin.

“Why, there’s no W in dandelions,” said Sarah, in surprise.

The young man drew the bill of fare from his pocket, and pointed to a line.

Sarah recognised the first card she had typewritten that afternoon. There was still the rayed splotch in the upper right-hand corner where a tear had fallen. But over the spot where one should have read the name of the meadow plant, the clinging memory of their golden blossoms had allowed her fingers to strike strange keys.

Between the red cabbage and the stuffed green peppers was the item:

“Dearest Walter, with hard-boiled egg.”
