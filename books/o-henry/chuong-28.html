The Hiding of Black Bill

Truyện 28 · Dấu vết của Bill Đen · 4.420 chữ · 273 từ

Từ in đậm được giải nghĩa ở cột bên phải. Chạm từ in đậm để tô sáng phần giải nghĩa. Mỗi truyện giải nghĩa độc lập.

BÀI ĐỌC
TỪ VỰNG

A lank, strong, red-faced man with a Wellington beak and small, fiery eyes tempered by flaxen lashes, sat on the station platform at Los Piños swinging his legs to and fro. At his side sat another man, fat, melancholy, and seedy, who seemed to be his friend. They had the appearance of men to whom life had appeared as a reversible coat—seamy on both sides.

  • lank (adj) /læŋk/gầy cao, lêu nghêu
  • Wellington beak (cụm từ)mũi khoằm to, giống mũi Công tước Wellington, vị tướng Anh nổi tiếng mũi khoằm
  • fiery (adj) /ˈfaɪəri/rực lửa, nảy lửa
  • temper (v) /ˈtempə/nghĩa trong bài: làm dịu bớt
  • flaxen (adj) /ˈflæksn/vàng nhạt như sợi lanh
  • to and fro (cụm từ)qua lại, đung đưa
  • melancholy (adj) /ˈmelənkəli/u sầu, rầu rĩ
  • seedy (adj) /ˈsiːdi/tiều tụy, lôi thôi, trông sa cơ
  • reversible coat (cụm từ)áo khoác mặc được cả hai mặt
  • seamy (adj) /ˈsiːmi/mặt trái, đầy đường may lồi; nghĩa bóng: tồi tệ. Ý đùa: đời họ mặt nào cũng là mặt trái

“Ain’t seen you in about four years, Ham,” said the seedy man. “Which way you been travelling?”

  • ain’t (tiếng lóng)(thông tục) = haven’t / am not / isn’t. Ở đây: “(I) haven’t seen you”

“Texas,” said the red-faced man. “It was too cold in Alaska for me. And I found it warm in Texas. I’ll tell you about one hot spell I went through there.

  • hot spell (cụm từ)đợt nắng nóng; ngầm ý: một phen “nóng”, gay go

“One morning I steps off the International at a water-tank and lets it go on without me. ’Twas a ranch country, and fuller of spite-houses than New York City. Only out there they build ’em twenty miles away so you can’t smell what they’ve got for dinner, instead of running ’em up two inches from their neighbors’ windows.

  • the International (cụm từ)tên chuyến tàu của tuyến đường sắt International–Great Northern ở Texas
  • water-tank (n) /ˈwɔːtə tæŋk/bồn nước bên đường ray, nơi tàu hơi nước dừng lấy nước
  • ’twas (tiếng lóng)(cách nói cũ) = it was
  • ranch (n) /rɑːntʃ/trại chăn nuôi lớn ở miền Tây nước Mỹ
  • spite-house (n) /ˈspaɪt haʊs/nhà xây cốt để chọc tức hàng xóm (chắn sáng, chắn lối)
  • ’em (tiếng lóng)(thông tục) = them
  • run up (cụm động từ)dựng vội, xây cấp tốc (nhà cửa)

“There wasn’t any roads in sight, so I footed it ’cross country. The grass was shoe-top deep, and the mesquite timber looked just like a peach orchard. It was so much like a gentleman’s private estate that every minute you expected a kennelful of bulldogs to run out and bite you. But I must have walked twenty miles before I came in sight of a ranch-house. It was a little one, about as big as an elevated-railroad station.

“There was a little man in a white shirt and brown overalls and a pink handkerchief around his neck rolling cigarettes under a tree in front of the door.

  • foot it (cụm từ)cuốc bộ, đi bộ
  • ’cross (tiếng lóng)= across: băng qua
  • shoe-top deep (cụm từ)ngập tới mũi giày
  • mesquite (n) /meˈskiːt/cây mai dương, cây bụi gai mọc khắp Tây Nam nước Mỹ
  • orchard (n) /ˈɔːtʃəd/vườn cây ăn quả
  • estate (n) /ɪˈsteɪt/điền trang, khu đất riêng rộng của nhà giàu
  • kennelful (n) /ˈkenlfʊl/cả một chuồng chó đầy (chữ tác giả ghép)
  • elevated-railroad station (cụm từ)ga tàu điện trên cao ở New York, rất nhỏ hẹp

“‘Greetings,’ says I. ‘Any refreshment, welcome, emoluments, or even work for a comparative stranger?’

“‘Oh, come in,’ says he, in a refined tone. ‘Sit down on that stool, please. I didn’t hear your horse coming.’

“‘He isn’t near enough yet,’ says I. ‘I walked. I don’t want to be a burden, but I wonder if you have three or four gallons of water handy.’

“‘You do look pretty dusty,’ says he; ‘but our bathing arrangements—’

“‘It’s a drink I want,’ says I. ‘Never mind the dust that’s on the outside.’

  • refreshment (n) /rɪˈfreʃmənt/đồ ăn uống cho lại sức
  • emolument (n) /ɪˈmɒljumənt/thù lao, bổng lộc. Chữ to tát, xin việc mà nói như xin chức quan
  • comparative stranger (cụm từ)người tương đối xa lạ; lối nói trịnh trọng để đùa

“He gets me a dipper of water out of a red jar hanging up, and then goes on:

“‘Do you want work?’

“‘For a time,’ says I. ‘This is a rather quiet section of the country, isn’t it?’

“‘It is,’ says he. ‘Sometimes—so I have been told—one sees no human being pass for weeks at a time. I’ve been here only a month. I bought the ranch from an old settler who wanted to move farther west.’

  • dipper (n) /ˈdɪpə/cái gáo múc nước

“‘It suits me,’ says I. ‘Quiet and retirement are good for a man sometimes. And I need a job. I can tend bar, salt mines, lecture, float stock, do a little middleweight slugging, and play the piano.’

  • retirement (n) /rɪˈtaɪəmənt/nghĩa trong bài: sự ẩn dật, sống xa người đời
  • tend bar (cụm từ)đứng quầy pha rượu
  • salt a mine (cụm từ)rắc quặng quý giả vào mỏ để lừa người mua, một trò lừa đảo
  • float stock (cụm từ)phát hành cổ phiếu (thường của công ty ma) để gọi vốn
  • middleweight slugging (cụm từ)đấm bốc hạng trung

“‘Can you herd sheep?’ asks the little ranchman.

“‘Do you mean have I heard sheep?’ says I.

“‘Can you herd ’em—take charge of a flock of ’em?’ says he.

  • herd (v) /hɜːd/chăn (gia súc). Chơi chữ: herd đọc giống heard (đã nghe)
  • ranchman (n) /ˈrɑːntʃmən/chủ trại chăn nuôi

“‘Oh,’ says I, ‘now I understand. You mean chase ’em around and bark at ’em like collie dogs. Well, I might,’ says I. ‘I’ve never exactly done any sheep-herding, but I’ve often seen ’em from car windows masticating daisies, and they don’t look dangerous.’

  • collie (n) /ˈkɒli/chó chăn cừu giống collie
  • masticate (v) /ˈmæstɪkeɪt/nhai. Chữ y học to tát cho chuyện cừu gặm cỏ

“‘I’m short a herder,’ says the ranchman. ‘You never can depend on the Mexicans. I’ve only got two flocks. You may take out my bunch of muttons—there are only eight hundred of ’em—in the morning, if you like. The pay is twelve dollars a month and your rations furnished. You camp in a tent on the prairie with your sheep. You do your own cooking, but wood and water are brought to your camp. It’s an easy job.’

  • herder (n) /ˈhɜːdə/người chăn gia súc
  • mutton (n) /ˈmʌtn/thịt cừu. Ở đây gọi đùa con cừu sống là “mutton”
  • ration (n) /ˈræʃn/khẩu phần lương thực được cấp
  • prairie (n) /ˈpreəri/đồng cỏ lớn ở Bắc Mỹ

“‘I’m on,’ says I. ‘I’ll take the job even if I have to garland my brow and hold on to a crook and wear a loose-effect and play on a pipe like the shepherds do in pictures.’

  • I’m on (tiếng lóng)tôi nhận lời, đồng ý
  • garland (v) /ˈɡɑːlənd/đội vòng hoa
  • crook (n) /krʊk/gậy đầu móc của người chăn cừu
  • loose-effect (cụm từ)áo thụng rộng kiểu mục đồng trong tranh
  • shepherd (n) /ˈʃepəd/người chăn cừu, mục đồng

“So the next morning the little ranchman helps me drive the flock of muttons from the corral to about two miles out and let ’em graze on a little hillside on the prairie. He gives me a lot of instructions about not letting bunches of them stray off from the herd, and driving ’em down to a water-hole to drink at noon.

  • corral (n) /kəˈrɑːl/bãi quây gia súc
  • graze (v) /ɡreɪz/gặm cỏ, thả cho ăn cỏ
  • water-hole (n) /ˈwɔːtə həʊl/vũng nước, hố nước cho thú uống

“‘I’ll bring out your tent and camping outfit and rations in the buckboard before night,’ says he.

“‘Fine,’ says I. ‘And don’t forget the rations. Nor the camping outfit. And be sure to bring the tent. Your name’s Zollicoffer, ain’t it?”

“‘My name,’ says he, ‘is Henry Ogden.’

“‘All right, Mr. Ogden,’ says I. ‘Mine is Mr. Percival Saint Clair.’

  • buckboard (n) /ˈbʌkbɔːd/xe ngựa bốn bánh thô sơ, sàn là tấm ván

“I herded sheep for five days on the Rancho Chiquito; and then the wool entered my soul. That getting next to Nature certainly got next to me. I was lonesomer than Crusoe’s goat. I’ve seen a lot of persons more entertaining as companions than those sheep were. I’d drive ’em to the corral and pen ’em every evening, and then cook my cornbread and mutton and coffee, and lie down in a tent the size of a tablecloth, and listen to the coyotes and whip-poor-wills singing around the camp.

  • Rancho Chiquito (cụm từ)(tiếng Tây Ban Nha) “Trại Nhỏ”, tên trại
  • the wool entered my soul (thành ngữ)nhại câu “the iron entered his soul” (nỗi đau thấm vào tâm can); đổi iron thành wool (lông cừu) để đùa
  • get next to (tiếng lóng)thấm vào tôi, làm tôi phát ngán. Chơi chữ với “getting next to Nature” (sống gần thiên nhiên)
  • lonesome (adj) /ˈləʊnsəm/cô đơn, hiu quạnh. “lonesomer” là dạng so sánh nói quê
  • Crusoe’s goat (cụm từ)con dê của Robinson Crusoe trên đảo hoang; hình ảnh cô độc tột cùng
  • pen (v) /pen/nhốt vào chuồng
  • cornbread (n) /ˈkɔːnbred/bánh mì bột ngô, món ăn bình dân miền Nam nước Mỹ
  • coyote (n) /kaɪˈəʊti/chó sói đồng cỏ Bắc Mỹ
  • whip-poor-will (n) /ˈwɪp pə wɪl/chim cú muỗi Bắc Mỹ, kêu đêm nghe như “uýp-pơ-uyl”

“The fifth evening, after I had corralled my costly but uncongenial muttons, I walked over to the ranch-house and stepped in the door. ‘Mr. Ogden,’ says I, ‘you and me have got to get sociable. Sheep are all very well to dot the landscape and furnish eight-dollar cotton suitings for man, but for table-talk and fireside companions they rank along with five-o’clock teasers. If you’ve got a deck of cards, or a parcheesi outfit, or a game of authors, get ’em out, and let’s get on a mental basis. I’ve got to do something in an intellectual line, if it’s only to knock somebody’s brains out.’

  • uncongenial (adj) /ˌʌnkənˈdʒiːniəl/không hợp tính, khó gần
  • suiting (n) /ˈsuːtɪŋ/vải may com-lê
  • rank along with (cụm từ)được xếp ngang hàng với
  • five-o’clock teaser (cụm từ)(cụm hiếm, nghĩa chưa chắc) có lẽ là đồ ăn nhẹ của buổi trà chiều năm giờ. Ý câu: cừu làm bạn chuyện trò thì nhạt nhẽo, chẳng ra gì
  • deck of cards (cụm từ)bộ bài (Mỹ)
  • parcheesi (n) /pɑːˈtʃiːzi/cờ cá ngựa (gốc Ấn Độ), trò chơi gia đình
  • Authors (cụm từ)trò chơi bài “Authors” về nhà văn và tác phẩm, rất phổ biến ở Mỹ thế kỷ 19
  • knock somebody’s brains out (thành ngữ)đập vỡ đầu ai. Chơi chữ với brain (đầu óc) và “việc trí tuệ”

“This Henry Ogden was a peculiar kind of ranchman. He wore finger-rings and a big gold watch and careful neckties. And his face was calm, and his nose-spectacles was kept very shiny. I saw once, in Muscogee, an outlaw hung for murdering six men, who was a dead ringer for him. But I knew a preacher in Arkansas that you would have taken to be his brother. I didn’t care much for him either way; what I wanted was some fellowship and communion with holy saints or lost sinners—anything sheepless would do.

“‘Well, Saint Clair,’ says he, laying down the book he was reading, ‘I guess it must be pretty lonesome for you at first. And I don’t deny that it’s monotonous for me. Are you sure you corralled your sheep so they won’t stray out?’

  • peculiar (adj) /pɪˈkjuːliə/khác thường, kỳ lạ
  • nose-spectacles (n) /ˈnəʊz spektəklz/kính kẹp mũi, không có gọng gài tai
  • outlaw (n) /ˈaʊtlɔː/kẻ sống ngoài vòng pháp luật
  • hang (v) /hæŋ/treo cổ (dạng chuẩn cho nghĩa này là hanged)
  • dead ringer (thành ngữ)người giống như đúc
  • preacher (n) /ˈpriːtʃə/người giảng đạo
  • fellowship (n) /ˈfeləʊʃɪp/tình bạn bè, sự giao du
  • communion (n) /kəˈmjuːniən/sự chuyện trò tâm giao; cũng là lễ ban thánh thể, nên ghép đùa với “holy saints”
  • sinner (n) /ˈsɪnə/kẻ có tội
  • sheepless (adj) /ˈʃiːpləs/không dính tới cừu (chữ tác giả chế)

“‘They’re shut up as tight as the jury of a millionaire murderer,’ says I. ‘And I’ll be back with them long before they’ll need their trained nurse.’

  • jury (n) /ˈdʒʊəri/bồi thẩm đoàn; vụ lớn thì bồi thẩm bị cách ly kín mít

“So Ogden digs up a deck of cards, and we play casino. After five days and nights of my sheep-camp it was like a toot on Broadway. When I caught big casino I felt as excited as if I had made a million in Trinity. And when H. O. loosened up a little and told the story about the lady in the Pullman car I laughed for five minutes.

  • dig up (cụm động từ)lục ra, tìm ra
  • casino (n) /kəˈsiːnəʊ/tên một trò chơi bài; “big casino” là lá mười rô, lá ăn điểm cao
  • toot (n) /tuːt/(lóng) cuộc chè chén vui chơi
  • Broadway (n) /ˈbrɔːdweɪ/đại lộ ở New York, khu nhà hát và giải trí sầm uất
  • Trinity (n) /ˈtrɪnəti/nhà thờ Trinity ở đầu phố Wall; ý chỉ thị trường chứng khoán
  • loosen up (cụm động từ)cởi mở, bớt dè dặt
  • Pullman car (cụm từ)toa giường nằm sang trọng của hãng Pullman

“That showed what a comparative thing life is. A man may see so much that he’d be bored to turn his head to look at a $3,000,000 fire or Joe Weber or the Adriatic Sea. But let him herd sheep for a spell, and you’ll see him splitting his ribs laughing at ‘Curfew Shall Not Ring Tonight,’ or really enjoying himself playing cards with ladies.

  • Joe Weber (n) /dʒəʊ ˈwebə/danh hài nổi tiếng ở New York khoảng năm 1900
  • split one’s ribs (thành ngữ)cười vỡ bụng
  • Curfew Shall Not Ring Tonight (cụm từ)bài thơ bi lụy nổi tiếng thế kỷ 19, hay bị đem ra ngâm; chẳng có gì đáng cười

“By-and-by Ogden gets out a decanter of Bourbon, and then there is a total eclipse of sheep.

  • by and by (cụm từ)lát sau, dần dần
  • decanter (n) /dɪˈkæntə/bình thủy tinh để rót rượu
  • Bourbon (n) /ˈbɜːbən/rượu whisky ngô của Mỹ
  • total eclipse (cụm từ)nhật thực toàn phần. Ý đùa: cừu bị quên sạch

“‘Do you remember reading in the papers, about a month ago,’ says he, ‘about a train holdup on the M. K. & T.? The express agent was shot through the shoulder and about $15,000 in currency taken. And it’s said that only one man did the job.’

  • holdup (n) /ˈhəʊldʌp/vụ chặn đường cướp
  • M. K. & T. (cụm từ)tuyến đường sắt Missouri–Kansas–Texas, gọi tắt là “the Katy”
  • express agent (cụm từ)nhân viên toa hàng gửi nhanh, giữ tiền và bưu kiện quý
  • currency (n) /ˈkʌrənsi/tiền giấy

“‘Seems to me I do,’ says I. ‘But such things happen so often they don’t linger long in the human Texas mind. Did they overtake, overhaul, seize, or lay hands upon the despoiler?’

  • overhaul (v) /ˌəʊvəˈhɔːl/nghĩa trong bài: đuổi kịp. Người kể nói dồn bốn động từ cùng nghĩa cho kêu
  • lay hands upon (cụm từ)tóm được, bắt được
  • despoiler (n) /dɪˈspɔɪlə/kẻ cướp bóc (chữ văn chương to tát)

“‘He escaped,’ says Ogden. ‘And I was just reading in a paper today that the officers have tracked him down into this part of the country. It seems the bills the robber got were all the first issue of currency to the Second National Bank of Espinosa City. And so they’ve followed the trail where they’ve been spent, and it leads this way.’

  • track down (cụm động từ)lần theo dấu vết mà tìm ra
  • first issue (cụm từ)đợt phát hành đầu tiên (tiền, tem…)

“Ogden pours out some more Bourbon, and shoves me the bottle.

  • shove (v) /ʃʌv/đẩy, xô (qua)

“‘I imagine,’ says I, after ingurgitating another modicum of the royal booze, ‘that it wouldn’t be at all a disingenuous idea for a train robber to run down into this part of the country to hide for a spell. A sheep-ranch, now,’ says I, ‘would be the finest kind of a place. Who’d ever expect to find such a desperate character among these songbirds and muttons and wild flowers? And, by the way,’ says I, kind of looking H. Ogden over, ‘was there any description mentioned of this single-handed terror? Was his lineaments or height and thickness or teeth fillings or style of habiliments set forth in print?’

“‘Why, no,’ says Ogden; ‘they say nobody got a good sight of him because he wore a mask. But they know it was a train-robber called Black Bill, because he always works alone and because he dropped a handkerchief in the express-car that had his name on it.’

  • ingurgitate (v) /ɪnˈɡɜːdʒɪteɪt/nốc, tọng. Chữ hiếm, dùng cho trịnh trọng để đùa
  • modicum (n) /ˈmɒdɪkəm/một chút, một ít
  • booze (n) /buːz/(lóng) rượu
  • disingenuous (adj) /ˌdɪsɪnˈdʒenjuəs/không thành thật. Người kể dùng sai cho kêu; ý là “chẳng phải ý dở”
  • desperate character (cụm từ)kẻ liều mạng, tội phạm nguy hiểm
  • single-handed (adj) /ˌsɪŋɡl ˈhændɪd/một mình, đơn thương độc mã
  • lineament (n) /ˈlɪniəmənt/đường nét khuôn mặt
  • teeth fillings (cụm từ)chỗ trám răng
  • habiliment (n) /həˈbɪlɪmənt/(cổ, trang trọng) quần áo, trang phục
  • set forth (cụm động từ)trình bày, nêu ra

“‘All right,’ says I. ‘I approve of Black Bill’s retreat to the sheep-ranges. I guess they won’t find him.’

“‘There’s one thousand dollars reward for his capture,’ says Ogden.

  • retreat (n) /rɪˈtriːt/sự rút về nơi ẩn náu; chốn ẩn cư
  • sheep-range (n) /ˈʃiːp reɪndʒ/bãi chăn thả cừu rộng

“‘I don’t need that kind of money,’ says I, looking Mr. Sheepman straight in the eye. ‘The twelve dollars a month you pay me is enough. I need a rest, and I can save up until I get enough to pay my fare to Texarkana, where my widowed mother lives. If Black Bill,’ I goes on, looking significantly at Ogden, ‘was to have come down this way—say, a month ago—and bought a little sheep-ranch and—’

  • fare (n) /feə/tiền vé tàu xe
  • Texarkana (n) /ˌteksɑːˈkænə/thị trấn nằm vắt ngang ranh giới Texas và Arkansas
  • significantly (adv) /sɪɡˈnɪfɪkəntli/nghĩa trong bài: một cách đầy ngụ ý

“‘Stop,’ says Ogden, getting out of his chair and looking pretty vicious. ‘Do you mean to insinuate—’

  • vicious (adj) /ˈvɪʃəs/hung dữ, hằn học
  • insinuate (v) /ɪnˈsɪnjueɪt/ám chỉ, nói bóng gió (điều xấu)

“‘Nothing,’ says I; ‘no insinuations. I’m stating a hypodermical case.
